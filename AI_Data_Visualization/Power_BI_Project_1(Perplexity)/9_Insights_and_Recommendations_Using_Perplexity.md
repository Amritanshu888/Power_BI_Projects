# Power BI — Using AI Tools to Analyze Report Visuals and Generate Insights

## 1. Objective of the Session

The first report page has now been created with multiple visuals.

The next step is not about creating another Power BI visual. Instead, the objective is to:

> **Use an AI tool to analyze the report/visuals and extract meaningful business insights, observations, and recommendations.**

The lecture demonstrates this using **Perplexity AI**.

The general workflow is:

```text
Power BI Report
      ↓
Take Screenshot
      ↓
Copy Screenshot
      ↓
Paste into AI Tool
      ↓
Ask AI to Analyze
      ↓
Get Observations
      ↓
Get Insights
      ↓
Get Recommendations
      ↓
Use Insights for Business Interpretation
```

---

# 2. Why Use AI for Power BI Analysis?

Creating a dashboard is only one part of data analysis.

After creating charts, we also need to answer questions such as:

* What does the data tell us?
* What relationships exist between variables?
* What are the important patterns?
* What are the major observations?
* Are there any unusual trends?
* What business implications can be derived?
* What recommendations can be made based on the data?

AI tools can assist with this interpretation.

Instead of manually examining every chart, you can provide the visual/report screenshot to an AI tool and ask it to identify important insights.

# 10. Price Sensitivity

Another insight generated by the AI concerns **price sensitivity**.

The AI observes that:

# 26. Critical Security and Data Privacy Consideration

This is one of the **most important points in the lecture**.

The instructor explicitly warns that you should **not automatically upload organizational data to external AI tools**.

Before using tools such as Perplexity or any other external AI service, check whether your organization permits it.

### If the organization does NOT allow it:

> **Do not upload or share the data.**

This includes potentially sensitive:

* Company data
* Customer information
* Business metrics
* Internal reports
* Confidential dashboards
* Proprietary information

---

The instructor explains that if your organization **does permit the use of such tools**, and allows you to provide data/report information to the tool for analysis, then you can use this workflow.

* Insights

* Explanations

---

# 28. Important Professional Principle

Always distinguish between:

### Technical capability

> "Can I upload this screenshot to the AI tool?"

and:

### Organizational permission

> "Am I allowed to upload this screenshot to the AI tool?"

The fact that an AI tool technically accepts an image does **not** mean that you are permitted to share your organization's data with it.

Therefore:

**Organization policy takes priority.**

# 30. Key Takeaways

### 1. Power BI visualization is only one part of analysis

Creating charts is not the end of the process.

You should also interpret what those charts mean.

### 2. AI can help interpret visuals

A screenshot of a chart/report can be provided to an AI tool, where permitted, to obtain:

* Observations
* Insights
* Recommendations
* Summaries

### 3. Prompt precisely

Instead of simply saying:

```text
Analyze this.
```

specify the desired output:

```text
Give me five major insights in points.
```

or:

```text
Analyze the relationship between price and carat and give recommendations.
```

### 4. Analyze both individual and complete reports

You can analyze:

* One specific chart for focused insights.
* The entire report page for broader insights.

### 5. Explore interesting insights further

If an AI-generated point is interesting, ask follow-up questions to understand the KPI or relationship in more detail.

### 6. Data privacy is critical

Never upload organizational data to external AI tools unless your organization's policies explicitly allow it.
